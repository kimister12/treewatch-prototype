1. THE PROBLEM

Teams cannot inspect every tree—so which tree do we check first?

4,825 Cases (2023)

Falling tree or branch cases in 2023, up 57% from 3,081 in 2021. Caused 10 deaths and 47 injuries in 2023.

~150 vs ~400

Few experts: about 150 ISA-certified arborists in Malaysia, versus about 400 in Singapore.

No Required Inventory & Unstructured Reports

Local authorities are not obliged to keep a tree inventory. Residents report by message with no photo, location, or priority.

Screening Alone Is Not Enough

Even Penang's AI flags (167 trees) still require an on-site inspection.

Sources: Soon (2024); New Straits Times (2024); Free Malaysia Today (2026) Slide 2 / 13
2. TARGET USER

Citizens scan. Handlers triage. Arborists decide when needed.

Primary: Citizens

Residents on the Ground

Notice a leaning tree, cracks, dead branches, or lifted roots. Challenge: No quick way to report, no proof they were heard, unsure how serious it is.

Secondary: Township Handlers

Management & Landscape Teams

Receive and act on reports. Challenge: Unstructured messages, no priority ranking, and scarce arborist time.

Escalation: Arborists

ISA-Certified Experts

Called in by the handler when needed. Challenge: Should only see the cases that really need an expert.

Ownership Routing: Tree ownership differs by location (developer, management body, or council), so each report is routed to the tree's owner (to confirm with SDP).

3. OUR SOLUTION

Citizens report. AI summarises. Handlers triage. Arborists decide.

1. Scan and Report

Citizen opens the app, scans the tree with guided photos, and sends a concern. GPS tags it automatically.

2. AI Report

AI checks the photos for visible warning signs and adds the tree's surroundings and the weather forecast.

3. Handler Triage

Handler sees a ranked report with photos, risk tier, and a suggested next step, then decides.

4. Arborist If Needed

Only escalated cases go to a certified arborist. The citizen is told the outcome.

5. HOW IT WORKS

Risk = Likelihood × Consequence × Storm Factor

Citizen Scan

Guided photos: whole tree, trunk base, canopy, and the concern. Quality check asks for a retake if blurry. GPS links report to a tree; duplicates are merged.

AI Report

Detects visible cues (lean, wound, dead branches, lifted roots) with confidence. Calculates Risk formula. Suggests: monitor, handler check, or arborist.

Handler, Then Arborist

Handler reviews ranked queue & storm watchlist, does a quick visual check, then escalates. Arborist gives final rating. Citizen gets updates.

Validation: An arborist rates ~100 trees. Target recall ≥90% for high-risk trees and ≥50% confirmed escalations.
Limits & Safety: Photos cannot show internal decay. AI never clears a tree on its own. Emergency warning if falling/blocking road.
Sources: Srivanit and Kaewkhow (2024); Velasquez-Camacho et al. (2023); Soon (2024) Slide 6 / 13

7. FEASIBILITY

Technical, Policy & Economic Viability

Can We Build It?

Phone-based web app, transparent rules first, photo AI as an assistant. No hardware. Working prototype already shows the flow.

Does Policy Support It?

The Minister urges councils to build inventories and employ arborists, and the National Landscape Department asks the public to report unsafe trees.

What Could Go Wrong?

False/duplicate reports merged by tree & reviewed by handler. AI never clears a tree. Privacy: minimal data, blurred faces & plates.

Illustrative Economics: Net benefit about RM25,200 a year | Pilot Budget: RM4,180 (Assumes 2 incidents avoided at RM15,000, 120 inspection hours saved at RM60, platform cost RM12,000).

9. WHY OUR TEAM SHOULD WIN

Protecting Sime Darby Property's Green Assets Without Blanket Felling

Falling trees are rising, arborists are scarce, and townships cannot see which trees worry residents. TreeWatch turns every resident into a sensor: they scan a tree, an AI report reaches the township handler, and an arborist is called only when needed. It needs no hardware, works on any phone, and a working prototype already demonstrates the flow. Targets are measurable in a three-month pilot: faster inspections, more high-risk trees checked before storms, and fewer unnecessary arborist visits.
